Transitioning to a private cloud shifts the scope of operational responsibilities. 
In a managed service model, a team configures services and monitors metrics, 
while issues involving the control plane or underlying hardware fall outside their purview. 
In contrast, in a private cloud, the control plane itself becomes the object that needs to be maintained. 
Patching, version upgrades, capacity planning, and fault location are all handled by the team itself, 
and the required skills have expanded from the application layer to virtualization, storage, networking, and databases. 
Therefore, the additional costs of a private cloud extend well beyond the price difference in raw computing power—they also include the long-term effort required to maintain the platform itself. 
This investment is only justified when the use of dedicated, exclusive infrastructure becomes a compliance requirement.
